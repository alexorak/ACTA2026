About these numbers

Everything comes from what each program entered on its SCA page, pulled 29 Sep 2026. Entries are self-reported, unaudited and undated; some are years old, and a few totals are clearly department-wide. Use them to shortlist and to shape questions, then confirm case logs with the program. Positions, salary, interviews, schedule, benefits and current contacts come from FREIDA (AMA program surveys, 2025 or 2026 cycle) and take priority over SCA where both exist.

NIH ranking

BRIMR 2025 rankings of NIH funding to anesthesiology departments (top 50, compiled by Roskoski and Parslow, created 10 Feb 2026). BRIMR ranks medical schools, so hospital-based programs such as the Harvard hospitals, Cleveland Clinic and Cedars-Sinai do not appear; Rutgers funding is credited to the university as a whole.

Institution NIH and SDN

"Inst. NIH" is the BRIMR 2025 rank of all NIH funding to the institution, which lets hospital-based programs be compared with medical schools. Applicant chatter comes from the Student Doctor Network ACTA 2026 thread and is anecdotal.

Score and tier

Composite of the criteria with usable data (prestige, volume per fellow, city fit, research, pay, structure), rescaled to 100. Tier is a rough competitiveness read for your profile; see the Shortlist and plan tab for method and caveats.

Sorting

Table sorting and dot sizes use the first number in each entry, so ">2000" sorts as 2000 and ranges use the low end. The dossier always shows the entry as written.

City data

Population: Census Bureau 2025 metro estimates. Cost and housing indexes: BEA Regional Price Parities 2024 (US average = 100; Cleveland is 2023). Taxes are top or flat rates as of 2025 and several states are cutting, so verify before deciding. Climate figures are approximate NOAA 1991-2020 normals; hazards, airports and transit are my summaries, not scores.

Metro size

My own grouping for quick filtering: major metro means one of the roughly 16 largest US metros; smaller city means a college town or a metro under about 1 million. Dot positions are approximate to the main hospital campus.

Shortlist and plan

Scored against your weights and stated preferences: prestige first, then a dense city with culture and food (with a penalty for the very largest, longest-commute metros), then pay. No partner or regional constraints; family in New York and partner's family in Vancouver noted as tiebreakers. Scores and tiers are also in the Explore table.

How the score works

Prestige (18)
BRIMR 2025 whole-institution NIH rank, banded 0 to 10. Hospital-based programs use their BRIMR hospital rank. Plus 1.5 for long-standing national heart-program brands, and a floor of 9.5 for the handful of top cardiac centers (Cleveland Clinic, Cedars, Mayo, Columbia, MGH, Brigham, Texas Heart, Stanford, Duke, Penn). The brand adjustments are my judgment.
Volume per fellow (20)
SCA CPB cases divided by FREIDA first-year positions (75%), plus transplants per fellow (25%). Self-reported and undated; unscored where missing.
City fit (12)
My 0 to 10 read of your preferences: culture, food, density and things to do, minus a commute penalty for NYC, Houston, Dallas and LA.
Research (7)
BRIMR anesthesiology department rank, with a bump for fellow abstracts per graduate (SCA).
Compensation (7)
FREIDA salary adjusted by BEA price parity, plus 1 for moonlighting, minus 1 for 70+ reported hours.
Structure (3)
FREIDA research rotation, research track and advanced-degree options.
Not scored
Placement (10), echo training (12), education (8) and culture (3) have no reliable public data, so they are left out and the rest is rescaled to 100. The completeness column shows how much of the weight is backed by data.

Where you stand, bluntly

Strengths: US MD, co-chief, MD/MBA plus an MS in physiology, real QI leadership, ASA 2026 presentations. Weaknesses: no first-author peer-reviewed cardiac work yet, a residency that is not a top-20 NIH anesthesia department, and a match that is tightening (unfilled spots fell from 25 to 12 to 7 over the last three cycles, per SDN). Your CA-2 ITE score and letters from recognized cardiac faculty will move you more than anything else on paper. Getting one cardiac case report or abstract accepted before invites go out in mid-December would help at every Reach program. Apply to roughly 18 to 22 programs and aim for 8 interviews. Complete the SF Match application in November, not March.

Shortlist: 20 programs

Reach

  1. Penn 88
    Top combined score: 433 CPB per fellow, Philadelphia fits you well, research track. Six seats and about 4.5 interviews per seat make it very selective; asks for ITE, MSPE and transcript.
  2. BIDMC 86
    Boston, Harvard teaching hospital, small class of 4, structural heart imaging curriculum. No moonlighting, and its CPB figure looks inconsistent with its cardiac total.
  3. Brigham 84
    Best city fit plus top brand and a required research block. Historically favors combined critical care applicants; cardiac-only invites came late (SDN).
  4. Columbia 84
    Highest price-adjusted pay (about $109k), 200 transplants, 45 fellow QI projects. Nine fellows share 1,500 CPB; SDN says fellows work very hard. NYC scale is a known tradeoff for you.
  5. MGH 84
    No. 1 NIH-funded hospital; Boston. No case volumes published, merging with Brigham anesthesia, and cardiac-only applicants heard nothing by mid-February last cycle.
  6. UCSF 83
    No. 2 NIH institution, 40 transplants per fellow, housing stipend. Only 2 seats, 72 reported hours a week, and the highest housing costs in the country after San Jose.
  7. Northwestern 77
    Chicago is one of your best city fits; strong heart program and six seats. Interviews dropped from 51 to 30 last cycle, which could mean a narrower invite list.

Target

    No. 6 NIH anesthesia department, 34 transplants per fellow, research track, Nashville. Probably a strong Target, not a lock.
  2. UPMC 80
    No. 4 NIH department, big transplant and VAD program, $6k tuition benefit, Pittsburgh is cheap. Good odds if your letters are strong.
  3. University of Washington 78
    Seattle, about 115 transplants a year, 2.5 hours to Vancouver. No moonlighting; Washington allows independent CRNA practice, which matters later, not in training.
  4. Cleveland Clinic 74

  5. University of Chicago 75
    Chicago, 3 seats, research track. Its FREIDA interview count last cycle (3) looks like a data error; confirm.
  6. Mount Sinai 73
    Ten seats and $110k salary; NYC. About 100 CPB per fellow is low for a class this size, so ask where the rest of the experience comes from.
  7. University of Colorado 73
    Denver, No. 11 NIH department, 90 transplants for 4 fellows. No moonlighting; supplemental statement required.
  8. Duke 72
    No. 3 NIH department, 128 fellow abstracts in 5 years, 14 seats. The class size dilutes volume to about 100 CPB per fellow; Durham is a middling city fit.

Launch cities

General reads for the metros of the top shortlisted programs, from knowledge of the market rather than job-board data. Verify with the SCA Career Center and group websites. CRNA opt-out status is from AANA and news reports: Massachusetts opted out in 2024, Ohio in July 2026; Washington and Colorado are long-standing opt-out states. Academic centers generally keep physician-led care teams either way.

Boston

Dense academic market (the Harvard hospitals, BU, Tufts, Lahey) with steady cardiac demand but academic pay and high housing costs. Massachusetts' 2024 opt-out has not changed care-team models at the big academic centers so far.

Philadelphia

Several academic heart programs (Penn, Jefferson, Temple, Einstein, Main Line) plus suburban groups; a good balance of culture and cost. Physician-led care teams predominate.

New York

The deepest cardiac job market in the country and your family is there, but academic salaries lag the cost of living and commutes are long.

Chicago

Big academic centers (Northwestern, UChicago, Rush, Loyola) and large consolidated private groups; good pay-to-cost ratio for a major city.

Seattle

UW, Swedish and Virginia Mason, with limited cardiac openings and high housing costs. Washington is an opt-out state with more independent CRNA practice outside academia. Close to your partner's family.

Nashville

Fast-growing hospital market (Vanderbilt, HCA headquarters, Ascension), no state income tax, a lively food and music scene.

Pittsburgh

UPMC dominates, with Allegheny as the second system; low costs, a strong cardiac and transplant base, fewer employers overall.

Cleveland and Denver

Cleveland: Clinic and UH dominate hiring; Ohio opted out in 2026. Denver: growing market with large private groups; Colorado is an opt-out state, mostly in rural settings.

Current-fellow interview guide

Core questions for every program

  1. How many bypass cases did you personally do by midyear, and how many transplants, VADs and ECMO cannulations?
  2. Roughly how many TEEs will you log this year, and are you on track for Advanced PTE without scrambling?
  3. In a typical week, how many cases are you doing with your own hands versus supervising a resident?
  4. Does that change across the year? When do you start running rooms or working a junior-attending shift?
  5. Who places the lines and does the TEE when a resident is in the room with you?
  6. Do CRNAs staff cardiac rooms here, and do they ever take cases you would want?
  7. How much of the teaching is protected, and do faculty actually teach in the room?
  8. Which electives did you really get, and what got cancelled because of staffing?
  9. Is there real research support (a mentor, data access, a statistician), or is it on your own time?
  10. Did the conference funding and days off actually come through this year?
  11. What does call look like in practice, and how often are you there past 12 hours?
  12. Is moonlighting allowed in practice, where, and at what rate?
  13. How do the cardiac surgeons treat fellows, especially when things go badly?
  14. Has anyone left, been remediated, or been unhappy in the last few years?
  15. Where did the last three classes get jobs, and did the department help?
